Metadata description – indicators_Leipzig.py

File: indicators_Leipzig.py
Case Study Area: Leipzig
Milestone: MS21 – Climate hazard indicators
Language: Python
Libraries: xarray, xclim, NumPy
Input: Daily/hourly gridded temperature, precipitation, humidity, and wind data (NetCDF)
Output: Climate indicators including warm spell duration, universal thermal climate index, annual mean temperature, maximum 5-day precipitation, and 95th percentile precipitation

Description:
This script implements multiple climate indicators for the Leipzig case study within the CARMINE-T2.4 project. It focuses on extreme temperature and precipitation events, as well as thermal comfort, to support climate impact assessment and adaptation planning in an urban central European context.

Methodology:

Warm Spell Duration Index (WSDI): Computes the number of days in warm spells relative to a baseline period. Users can either compute the 90th percentile internally or load a pre-computed percentile file.

Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI): Integrates temperature, humidity, and wind to assess human thermal stress.

Air Temperature (air_t): Aggregates daily data to annual mean temperatures for climate monitoring.

Maximum 5-day Precipitation (rx5day): Calculates extreme short-term precipitation events, resampling daily totals over a specified frequency.

95th Percentile of Precipitation (pr95prctile): Computes the 95th percentile of precipitation, handling missing or negligible values, for extreme event analysis.

Key features:

Handles multi-dimensional xarray datasets efficiently

Supports flexible baseline periods for percentile-based indicators

Provides both thermal stress and precipitation extremes assessment

Reproducible and modular for integration into broader CSA workflows

Intended use:
The script is designed for technical users analyzing climate extremes in Leipzig. It can be integrated into the CARMINE repository workflow for indicator computation, post-processing, and visualization, supporting standardized evaluation across multiple datasets.
